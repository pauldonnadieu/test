# Horizon

Runs at the end of the weekly review, or on request. Three or four lines of output, never a
second review.

## What to look at

**Approaching.** Anything mentioned in a check-in or filed in `reference/` with a date or a
dependency. What needs preparation, and has it been prepared?

**Gone quiet.** A goal not mentioned in any check-in for two weeks or more. Name it, do not scold
it: "`goals/health.md` has not come up for nineteen days" is useful, and "you have been
neglecting your health goal" is a reason to stop reading the review.

**About to be forgotten.** The mechanical set, which is where most of the value is because none
of it is memorable: an attestation within thirty days of its first birthday; the quarterly
restore drill; a measurement window about to close; a parked item whose trigger condition has now
been met; a skill approaching ninety days unfired.

**Worth deciding now.** Something that will otherwise be decided under time pressure later.
Naming it early is cheap and the decision is usually better.

## The rules

**Prepare rather than remind.** Where something needs doing and can be prepared, invoke
`skills/prepare/` and hand over the artefact. A horizon section that is purely a list of
obligations is a to-do list the user did not ask for.

**Do not manufacture.** If nothing is approaching, write "nothing on the horizon" and stop. A
forward section that always finds something is generating noise to justify itself, exactly like a
review that always finds a bottleneck.

**Raise once.** Something named this week is not named again next week unless it has moved or is
now urgent. Repetition is how a coach gets muted.

**Respect the parked list.** `reference/R7-after-v1.md` holds things deliberately deferred with
trigger conditions. If a trigger has been met, that is worth surfacing. If it has not, the item
is not "outstanding", it is parked, and the difference matters.

## Output

Bullets, in the review, under "Coming up". No headers, no preamble, no closing encouragement.
