# Bottleneck analysis

Run this when a goal has not moved for three consecutive weeks, when the weekly review needs
output 3, or on request.

**Work in order. Stop at the first question the evidence actually answers.** Do not assemble a
comprehensive diagnosis: a list of twelve contributing factors is not a finding, it is a way of
avoiding one.

## The ladder

1. **Is the goal still right?** People outgrow goals and keep the file. The honest answer here
   retires more goals than it fixes.
2. **Is the strategy sound?** Would this approach work for someone with unlimited capacity?
3. **Is it realistic given actual constraints?** Check it against `goals/` capacity, not
   against an ideal week.
4. **Is it environmental: where, or when?** The most under-diagnosed one. Office days versus
   home days, mornings versus evenings, alone versus with the family in the house.
5. **Is it energy or capacity?** Check the energy scores against the days it failed.
6. **Is there too much complexity?** A five-step routine fails where a one-step routine holds.
7. **Is there friction: small repeated costs?** The gym bag not being packed. The document not
   being open. Each costs thirty seconds and each is where the attempt dies.
8. **Is the task being avoided, and what specifically is uncomfortable?** Not "they are
   avoiding it" but which twenty seconds of it they are avoiding.
9. **Is there a pattern across goals?** The same failure in three places is one problem, not
   three.
10. **Are they attempting too many things at once?** Check the active goal count against the
    cap in `skills/goals/`.
11. **Is it self-sabotage?** Only with `skills/patterns/`, and only with its evidence rules.
12. **Can the AIOS remove part of the problem outright**, rather than helping them through it?

Question 12 is asked last and is the one worth most. Helping someone do a thing every week is
worse than making the thing unnecessary.

## Output

One named constraint. The evidence. One intervention small enough to happen this week.

Not a diagnosis document. Three paragraphs at most.

## Rules

**Do not reduce every problem to motivation. Do not reduce every problem to circumstances.**
Determine what is actually happening. Both reflexes are comfortable and both are usually wrong.

**Find the actual constraint before prescribing the solution.**

**When the constraint is genuine, do not prescribe more discipline.** Removal, reduction,
automation, delegation, substitution, simplification, resequencing, or a temporary minimum
viable version. In roughly that order of preference, because the earlier ones do not depend on
the user having a good week.

**A goal that has been a project for six months usually needs to become a system.** That is a
finding, not a failure.
