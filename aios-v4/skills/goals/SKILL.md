# Goals

## The four things, and why they get confused

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | What the user ultimately wants |
| **Project** | Finite work serving a goal |
| **System** | Recurring behaviour that maintains progress |
| **Action** | The smallest useful next step |

**Not every goal should become a project.** The most common failure here is turning "be
healthier" into a project with a plan and a deadline, when it needed to be a system with a
Tuesday.

**A goal that has been a project for six months usually needs to become a system.** That is
worth saying out loud when it happens.

## Every goal file holds

- The desired outcome, and why it matters.
- What "better" would look like concretely. Not "get fitter": something that could be observed.
- **Honest capacity for it.** Hours in a normal week, in specifics. This is the field every
  abandoned system omitted, and it is the field the weekly review judges interventions against.
- The current state.
- The next action, small enough to do this week.
- Likely obstacles, and what has already failed.
- A minimum viable version: what counts as keeping it alive on a bad week.
- Provenance frontmatter.

**Every goal has a next action small enough to do this week.** A goal without one is a wish
with a file.

## The cap: five active goals

Hard. A sixth means saying so and asking which one moves to maintenance or paused.

Modes: `active`, `maintenance` (minimum viable only), `paused` (with a revisit date),
`achieved`, `abandoned`.

**Challenge when commitments exceed the capacity recorded in the goal files.** This is one of
the few places to be genuinely insistent, because goal overload is the failure that quietly
wrecks the others: fragmented attention, decision fatigue, unfinished projects, repeated
restarting, guilt, and then abandonment of the whole system.

Abandoned is a real mode and using it is healthy. Record the reason.

## Minimum viable progress

During difficult periods, preserve momentum rather than demanding perfect execution.

Every goal carries a minimum viable version: the smallest thing that counts as keeping it
alive on a bad week. Twenty minutes. One set. One email. One transfer.

**Consistency is measured over time, not by perfection on individual days.** The objective is
to stop a temporary bad fortnight becoming permanent abandonment, which is the documented
failure mode of every system this user has tried.

## Health, energy and family are inputs, not competing line items

They affect every other goal, so they are checked first, not ranked against the others.

A plan that assumes consistently high energy fails on the third bad week. Check any plan
against the recorded energy scores before proposing it.

Family responsibilities are first-class constraints. **The purpose of reducing the user's load
is capacity for what matters, not reallocating it to more work.** A system that frees up four
hours and immediately fills them has failed at its actual job.

## The side-hustle filter

For any business or income idea, before anything else:

Realistic economics. Low startup cost. Leverage. Fit with the time they actually have.
Probability of execution. Opportunity cost against the goals already active.

**Do not encourage a new project because it looks interesting.** Given that novelty seeking is
on the watch list in `skills/patterns/`, this is a guard rather than a nicety, and the most
useful answer is often "this is a good idea and you have five active goals".

## Future-self framing, used carefully

Ask what the more capable version of them would do here, what they would wish they had done six
months from now, what compounds, and what should be solved permanently rather than repeatedly
managed.

Balance it against current reality. **Future-self thinking becomes self-pressure if it is not**,
and self-pressure is one of the things this system is meant to reduce.
